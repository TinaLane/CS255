# CS255

CS 255: System Analysis and Design

DriverPass Project Reflection

Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?

The client was DriverPass, a company that wanted to help students better prepare for their DMV driving tests through online learning, practice tests, and behind-the-wheel instruction. DriverPass needed a web-based system that would allow customers to register, purchase training packages, schedule driving lessons, and track their progress. The system also needed to support employees with managing instructors, vehicles, reservations, customer information, and administrative reports. The goal was to create a centralized, secure, and accessible system that would improve business operations and the customer experience.

What did you do particularly well?

I think one of my strengths in this project was translating the client's business needs into organized system requirements and visual designs. I carefully considered the different responsibilities of customers, instructors, the secretary, management, and IT personnel to ensure the system would support each user's needs. I also focused on the scheduling process, particularly checking instructor and vehicle availability to prevent overlapping reservations. Creating the UML diagrams helped me connect the system's requirements to the way its components and processes would function.

If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?

If I could revise one part of my work, I would further develop the UML class diagram. Although the diagram identifies the main classes, attributes, methods, and relationships, I would like to refine how the classes communicate and handle more complex situations. For example, I would expand the design to better represent reservation changes, payment processing, and activity tracking. This would provide developers with a clearer understanding of how information moves through the system and how different components work together.

How did you interpret the user's needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user's needs when designing?

I began by reviewing the DriverPass interview transcript to identify the client's goals, business processes, and expectations. I then organized those needs into functional and nonfunctional requirements that guided my system design. For example, the client's need to prevent scheduling conflicts became a requirement to verify instructor and vehicle availability before confirming reservations. I used UML use case, activity, sequence, and class diagrams to illustrate how the system would support these requirements. Considering user needs is essential because a system can function correctly from a technical perspective but still fail if it does not solve the problems users experience. Understanding those needs helps create software that is practical, efficient, and easier to use.

How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?

My approach to software design begins with understanding the problem before deciding how to build the solution. I believe it is important to identify the users, understand their responsibilities, document the requirements, and consider how different parts of the system will interact. This project reinforced the value of using UML diagrams to examine a system from multiple perspectives. In future projects, I would continue using requirements analysis, process modeling, object-oriented modeling, and regular stakeholder feedback to identify potential problems before development begins. I would also focus on maintaining clear documentation so developers, designers, and clients have a shared understanding of the system throughout the development process.
